Lever A — pre-warmed lookalike / redirect domains

Launch in 48–72 hours, not 4–5 weeks

A standing inventory of lookalike domains (e.g. get-acmecorp.com, 301-redirected to the client's real site) with mailboxes that are already warm. When a client signs, domains are assigned from inventory, copy is personalized, and sending starts at full volume — the 3-week warm-up that normally gates every launch was paid in advance, continuously, as a background process.

Lever B — owned raw-code scrapers replace per-credit tools

Cost per 1,000 enriched contacts

Per-credit data tools price each record at retail. Owned scrapers (Google Maps, SERP) price it at proxies + compute, and a bulk LinkedIn corpus prices it at amortized acquisition. The fixed cost moves to engineering time; the marginal cost per record collapses.

  • Per-credit tools make data an expense that scales with revenue. Owned scrapers make it a fixed cost that amortizes toward zero — which is exactly what an 80%+ margin at $150K MRR requires.
  • The trade is capex-for-opex: a few weeks of engineering and ~$1.5K/mo of proxy/server spend replaces $10–40K/mo of credit burn at this lead volume.
  • The moat is operational, not technical. Scraper code is easy; keeping it running against anti-bot systems every week is the 2-person team's real job.

Why the two levers compound

Lever A buys revenue speed

Pre-warmed domains collapse time-to-first-lead from ~5 weeks to ~3 days. That makes pay-per-lead pricing sellable at all — and pay-per-lead is what lets 2 people charge for outcomes instead of hours, decoupling MRR from headcount.

Lever B buys margin

Owned scrapers collapse the cost of goods. At 300 leads/mo built from ~150K raw records, credit tools would burn $10–40K/mo; owned infrastructure burns ~$1.5K. That spread is the 80%+ margin — and it's also where the marketing hyperbole comes from: quoting the amortized corpus cost as if it were the live cost.
